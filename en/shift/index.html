How we teach

This is how the lecture runs

  • We start from the audience’s own work

    Students pick tasks from their interests; staff from their real job.

  • Everyone makes one thing

    People new to AI take home one piece of their own work. Regular AI users take home a request–check–fix routine.

  • Checking and fixing, too

    We practise where to check an AI answer and how to fix it.

What we do

We teach first, and build for you if you want

Lectures come first. We also find instructors, and automate one task for a company that took a lecture.

  1. Hands-on AI lecture

    A hands-on lecture where staff make one piece of work from their own job.

    Ask about this lecture · Hands-on AI lecture ↗
    Who it is for · what you keep · steps
    Who it is for
    Companies, public bodies and schools that want staff to use AI in their actual work
    What you keep
    One piece of work per person, and a request–check–fix routine they can reuse
    Agreed first
    Learning goals and audience, the tools allowed and any extra cost, and material that must not be used in the exercises
    Steps
    1. Pick practice tasks from the audience’s real work
    2. Each person makes one piece of work from their own material
    3. Practise checking and fixing what the AI produced
    Example
    “30 office staff · lots of reports and email · new to AI”

  2. AI career lectures for schools

    Students see how AI is changing their field, then write a one-page plan of what to prepare now.

    Ask about this lecture · AI career lectures for schools ↗
    Who it is for · what you keep · steps
    Who it is for
    Schools that want their career education to reflect how AI is changing work
    What you keep
    A one-page plan for each student — what AI is changing in their field, and what they will prepare
    Agreed first
    Year group and class size, lesson time, and the devices and tools students can use
    Steps
    1. See how AI is changing work in each student’s field of interest
    2. Pick the skills that kind of work needs, and what to start on now
    3. Write a one-page career plan and talk it through
    Example
    “Second-year high school · careers class · everyone’s interests differ”

  3. Finding an instructor

    We find one for your goal and audience. You know who is coming before you sign.

    Ask for an instructor · Finding an instructor ↗
    Who it is for · what you keep · steps
    Who it is for
    Schools, companies and institutions that have a topic but struggle to find an AI or AI-career instructor who fits their audience
    What you keep
    An instructor who fits your audience, with a profile sheet — who they teach and for what kind of work, what they have made themselves, samples they can share, and what stays after the class
    Agreed first
    Running the day, substitute instructors, cancellation, and questions after the lecture
    Steps
    1. Pin down the goal and the audience
    2. Show the instructor’s profile sheet and samples
    3. Sign once instructor, date and terms are confirmed
    Example
    “High school careers week · the whole second year · a talk in the hall”

  4. AI work automation

    We automate one task you repeat each week, with a human check and an alert if it stops.

    Ask about automation · AI work automation ↗
    Who it is for · what you keep · steps
    Who it is for
    Small companies paying fixed costs for the same entry, sorting and reporting that people repeat every week
    What you keep
    One working automation, and a routine that alerts you and hands over to a person when it stops
    Agreed first
    What the task costs in time and money today, where a person checks, and who fixes it after handover
    Steps
    1. Map the current workflow together in a half-day review
    2. Demonstrate one automation on the spot and send a proposal
    3. Agree scope and cost, build it, and check it every month
    Example
    “Copying order emails into the ledger · daily · one person”
    1. Lecture

      First, look together at what your company can hand to AI and what should stay with people.

    2. Half-day review

      We map the workflow with you, demonstrate one automation on the spot, then send a proposal with scope and cost. You decide whether to build after reading it, and you can stop after the review.

    3. Build one task

      Scope and price are agreed up front, then we automate one task — including where a person checks and how it alerts you when it stops.

    4. Monthly care

      Each month we check it still runs and adjust it when the work or the services it uses change. Time and cost saved are measured before and after under the same conditions, and we report them to you.

Pricing

No price list: we agree the scope first

For each request we agree the audience and scope, then quote. What the price depends on, we tell you up front.

  • Lectures

    Set by audience and size, length and sessions, practice material and venue. If we assign another instructor, their terms are confirmed too.

  • Automation build

    Scope and price for one task are set in a half-day review. They depend on exceptions and connected services; the price is based on the cost the automation saves.

  • Monthly care

    We agree how often we check and what we fix, month by month. External service fees may be extra, and a big change in the work means a new scope and cost.
