SYNK PATHStudy · work · settling in

Studying, working and living in Korea, prepared with you

From packing your bags to settling into life in Korea, we are preparing to work through the Korean you need, the requirements, interviews and daily life with you, one step at a time. SYNK LAB takes care of the Korean — SYNK WORLD’s TOPIK practice is open now, and we are preparing to open the one-year course.

01 · SeoulLive · Free

Three hours in Seoul, what will you do?

Pick what you feel like doing and how much time you have, and your day is planned — no sign-up needed. It uses real places around Jongno, Bukchon and Seochon, checked against official information, and you can swap out any stop you don’t like. The planner is in Korean.

Plan a day (Korean) ↗
  1. 1

    What do you feel like today?

    Pick up to three: a good meal, a cup of tea, an exhibition, palaces, alleys or a market.

  2. 2

    How much time do you have?

    Choose anything from an hour to a full day, and it tells you first how many places will fit.

  3. 3

    Here is your day

    The order of stops, arrival times, walking and time at each place, all in one view.

Check opening hours and prices before you visit. We do not take bookings or payments or give live directions, and for now it plans days around Jongno, Bukchon and Seochon only.

02 · StudyIn preparation

Preparing to study in Korea, around your major and career

We are getting ready to compare schools with you by the conditions that matter for your major and career, and to set a timeline that fits your Korean level and the time you have.

  • Study plan

    Organize your goals, budget, Korean level and application timing, compare candidate schools and set a timeline.

  • Applications and interviews

    Polish the application documents you have written, practise for interviews, and check deadlines and what to do before you leave.

  • Portfolio

    Show what you have learned and done through your study plan and your own work. Only material you have checked and agreed to share is ever shared.

The scope, schedule and cost of support are agreed after a consultation. LAB’s Korean course→

03 · WorkIn preparation

For the job you want, start with the requirements

Working in Korea takes a different visa, qualifications and level of Korean in each field. For fields that need a qualification, such as care work or manufacturing, we are preparing to set out what you need and by when, step by step and from your point of view.

  • Visa, qualification and Korean requirements by field
  • Qualification training and exams follow the accredited bodies’ guidance
  • Reminders for exam and document deadlines, and interview practice

PATH helps you find out the requirements; it does not guarantee a visa or a job.

04 · VRLive

Rehearse your first interview before it happens

A 60–90 second practice: hear questions from three interviewers at a caregiver interview and answer them in Korean. Try it on a PC first, or use voice answers and VR mode in a supported Quest browser.

Start the interview (Korean) ↗

Practice does not count as work experience or a qualification. VR needs a supported headset and browser plus microphone permission, and it has not yet been tested on a physical Quest. Visa interview and university-life practice are in preparation.

05 · HousingIn preparation

Finding a place to live in Korea

We are preparing to help people coming to Korea for study or work choose where to live and start daily life.

  • Conditions and comparison

    Set out your length of stay, budget and distance to school or work, and compare the homes you can choose from.

  • Moving in

    Find out what you need at the start, such as move-in dates, what to bring and how the facilities work.

  • When something goes wrong

    Get help reporting problems with the building or daily life to the operator.

Partner operators, areas and costs are not decided yet, and we are not taking move-in bookings. Contracts and the running of buildings are handled by the operator.

06 · Settle TogetherIn preparation · 2027 pilot

Your first 30 days of to-dos, seen through with you

Settle Together stays beside people who have just arrived in Korea, from the first day until they have settled in. It does not stop at telling you what must be done in the first 30 days — registering as a foreign resident, opening a bank account, health insurance — but checks with each person that it is done.

  • To-dos

    The first 30 days, today’s to-dos

    The app tells you what to do and by when, such as registering as a foreign resident, opening a bank account and paying health insurance. You mark each task done yourself, and if Korean is still hard for you, a manager goes with you.

  • Counselling

    AI first, then a manager

    Ask a question and an AI answers first in several languages; anything a person needs to check is taken over by a manager who counsels in plain Korean. Visa, administrative and legal problems go straight to the existing counselling services, and anything involving life or safety starts with the emergency numbers.

  • Meetups

    One team with Koreans

    International students and Korean students and residents play a season league every other week on mixed teams, and there are cooking and language-exchange meetups. Teams are reshuffled every season, and dinner after the match is alcohol-free.

  • QUARTERLY

    Quarterly settlement indicators

    Every quarter we measure the share who finished the required paperwork of the first 30 days, how long it took to first answer a question, the share of people who lost touch and were found and helped, the share who came back to meetups, and how many Koreans took part. Partner institutions receive only totals in which no one can be identified.

  • PRINCIPLES

    Principles we keep

    You take part only with your consent, and we do not take foreign resident registration numbers or passport numbers. What you tell us is not used for enforcement, and participants pay nothing for the core support.

  • PILOT 2027

    The 2027 pilot

    From March to August 2027 (spring semester and summer break), we are preparing a pilot in which a dedicated manager and senior international students look after 30–50 people. App notifications and the AI first reply will be open to up to 200 people once the app is released (target: February).

The app screens are mockups in Korean, and the names, numbers and dates in them are examples. Existing institutions keep their counselling and education as they are; we add follow-through to the end and settlement indicators. We are not taking applications yet.
