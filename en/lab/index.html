What we do differently

  1. 01

    The scene waits for you

    Not a drama you watch: the next scene only opens when you say your line.

  2. 02

    Characters react, not scores

    Get it wrong and the other character asks again, in character; the second time you hear the model reading; the third time it lets you through.

  3. 03

    One note, and only when sure

    Endings and politeness, length, then missing words — one note at a time. If it is not sure, there is no note.

  4. 04

    Measured on unseen lines

    It only counts as “done on your own” when you say the same expression to someone else, in a new situation.

TOPIK 4

The benchmark for studying in Korea: TOPIK Level 4

We aim for Level 4 with life after admission in mind. Some universities admit students at Level 3, but Level 4 removes many obstacles.

  1. 01

    Graduation

    Some universities require Level 4 or higher to graduate.

  2. 02

    Scholarships

    Some universities offer different scholarships at Level 3 and Level 4.

  3. 03

    Part-time work

    For third- and fourth-year undergraduates, the Korean requirement for part-time work is Level 4.

CourseIn preparation · Preparing to open

One year to get ready for study and life in Korea

An in-person one-year course centred on TOPIK Level 4, covering what you need to follow classes at a Korean university and handle daily life.

  1. 01

    Korean

    Use the grammar and expressions from the textbooks in conversation and games.

  2. 02

    Judgement and teamwork

    Explain the reasons behind your choices in team projects, in Korean.

  3. 03

    Portfolio

    Collect the writing you drafted and revised and the roles you took on.

  4. 04

    Interviews and daily life

    Practise visa interviews and unfamiliar everyday situations before they happen.

In every 90-minute class, 30 minutes are for talking together

Sixty minutes of lessons and practice, thirty minutes of group speaking. In that time you decide what you think first, hear your classmates’ reasons, and then explain your final choice yourself.

  1. Decide what you think — Choose before hearing anyone else’s answer.
  2. Discuss together — Ask for reasons and agree how everyone takes part.
  3. Explain your decision — Say what you kept and what you changed, and why.

  • Conversation with native speakers

    Fix pronunciation and honorifics, and answer unexpected questions in your own words.

  • English classes

    From introductions to reading, email and presentations, taught separately at your level.

  • Warming up with K-pop

    Learn rhythm with a trained dance instructor and pick up movement verbs like “turn” and “stop” with your body.

Research and school examples behind the movement classes

We drew on work on exercise and memory (John Ratey of Harvard Medical School; Naperville Central High School), Finland’s Schools on the Move programme, and research on gesture and vocabulary memory (Max Planck Institute, 2015). These informed the class design; they are not measurements of LAB classes.
