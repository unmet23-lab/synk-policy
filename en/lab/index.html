LAB’s educational philosophy

Learn Korean.
Learn to think for yourself.

LAB aims to prepare learners for studying in Korea and for life beyond it. TOPIK Level 4 is a clear goal, supported by experience using Korean in real situations. We want to help learners use AI while developing the ability to explain their own thinking and evaluate the results.

Why TOPIK Level 4?

A goal for studying in Korea:
TOPIK Level 4.

We chose Level 4 with university classes and daily life in mind. Building on Level 3 Korean, learners practice reading more complex materials and explaining their opinions.

Part-time work · Language standards and permitted hours by year＋

The Korean-language standard for part-time work is TOPIK Level 3 for first- and second-year undergraduates, and Level 4 for third- and fourth-year undergraduates. For third- and fourth-year students, the guidance allows 10 weekday hours without the language requirement and 25 hours with it. Preferential conditions, such as university accreditation or grades, may allow 30 hours. Alternatives to TOPIK are also recognized. Before starting work, students need confirmation from their university and immigration permission for part-time employment.

Scholarships · How Level 3 and Level 4 benefits differ＋

Konyang University’s tuition scholarship guidance for new and transfer students lists 30% for TOPIK Level 3 and 50% for Level 4—a difference of 20 percentage points at the same university. These benefits do not apply to every university. Check the latest admissions guide for the relevant semester, admissions track, and scholarship renewal conditions.

Graduation · Language requirements after admission＋

The Korean proficiency needed for admission may differ from that required for graduation. For example, Sangmyung University lists TOPIK Level 4 or higher for Korean graduation certification in general academic fields. Requirements and accepted alternatives vary by university, department, and admission year. LAB chose Level 4 as a goal that looks beyond admission.

TOPIK results can serve as evidence of Korean proficiency in employment and immigration reviews. Alongside Level 4 preparation, we plan practice in explaining experience at interviews and clarifying workplace tasks. Job-seeking status (D-10) and employment status (such as E-7) are different categories, with separate requirements for education, occupation, contracts, and more. Level 4 alone does not secure a work visa or resolve immigration matters.

How are the 30 minutes used?＋

This is group activity time within the 90-minute class. It is not an extra 30 minutes or 30 minutes of solo speaking per student. Learners practice speaking, listening, asking questions, and coordinating, while the teacher checks that the conversation is not dominated by a few students. Task difficulty and support are adjusted to the learners’ levels, while the group speaking time remains fixed.

These are operating guidelines for the regular classes currently in preparation. Cooperation, consideration, and judgment are skills we aim to develop, not measured improvement results.

What if my Korean and English levels are different?＋

We assess Korean and English separately. If you are comfortable speaking Korean but new to English, for example, English classes begin with basic expressions. In each class, we review what learners say and write and prepare further practice for difficult pronunciation or expressions. Learners then try the same expressions in a different situation to check their understanding.

Which programs include these classes?＋

We are preparing a program focused on regular Korean classes and another that also includes native Korean conversation and English. Students who want conversation and English classes can choose a program that includes them. Check enrollment information or ask us about opening dates, teachers, class frequency, and what is included.

How does this support TOPIK, studying abroad, and career preparation?＋

TOPIK · Put what you understand into speech and writing — Use vocabulary and grammar from Korean classes to answer a native teacher’s questions and explain your opinions. Write up the conversation afterward to compare spoken expressions with the written style used in test answers. Listening, reading, and writing are also practiced separately in the test formats. Studying abroad · Ask questions, get help, and take part — Practice asking professors questions, agreeing on roles in team assignments, and checking information at university offices in Korean. English tasks include explaining opinions to international peers and reading short materials in your field or instructions for English-taught classes. Practice asking again and checking anything you did not understand. Career preparation · Explain experience and clarify work — Describe your project role and the problems you solved in Korean, then answer follow-up interview questions. Students preparing for careers that require English also practice introductions, work emails, summaries, and short presentations. Beyond rehearsing answers, tasks include asking about unclear instructions and confirming schedules and results.

What will students take away?＋

We aim to help students build a portfolio of Korean request emails and opinion pieces they have written and revised, Korean and English introductions and project descriptions, expressions refined through teacher feedback, team roles, and presentation materials. Recording original wording alongside the reasons for revisions helps students explain their progress. Teachers use this work to suggest further practice, and students can revisit useful materials before a new semester or interview. The length and difficulty of the work depend on the student’s starting level and chosen program.

How are both languages used in one project?＋

For example, in a project creating a university guide for new international students, learners ask the university questions in Korean and organize the information they confirm. With an English teacher, they identify useful information in English guides and write a message for students from other countries. With a native Korean teacher, they explain the guide aloud and answer unexpected questions. Students compare AI translations or drafts with verified sources and explain why they chose the final wording.

Do English and native-speaker conversation help with TOPIK?＋

TOPIK II assesses Korean listening, reading, and writing; speaking is a separate test. We design listening, reading, and writing tasks that revisit vocabulary and expressions used in conversation to support test study. English is not assessed in TOPIK. LAB’s English classes aim to help learners understand English materials and communicate in another language; we do not claim that English classes themselves raise TOPIK scores.

Vocabulary research in cognitive and brain science

Max Planck Institute

In a 2015 study, participants who performed gestures matching the meanings of unfamiliar words they heard remembered the words better. Meaningful pictures also helped memory.

Explore gestures and vocabulary research＋

Researchers at the Max Planck Institute for Human Cognitive and Brain Sciences had participants learn unfamiliar words from an artificial language in several ways. Performing gestures or viewing pictures that matched the meanings helped vocabulary memory compared with listening alone. Watching a movement and performing it produced different results; the connection between the word’s meaning and the movement matters.

How LAB designs its classesWe choose gestures and images that fit the meanings of the Korean being learned. Students use the expressions to describe the order of movements or explain them to classmates, then use them again in cultural projects after class.

How do you practice in the games?＋
  • We create games with clear roles and goals, based on grammar from Synapse Core and conversations from Synapse Talk. Scenes and characters help students understand the situation and take part themselves.
  • In the website’s email-writing experience, you write first and can ask for hints if you get stuck. Compare your first and revised drafts to see what changed.
  • We are developing a way to record sentences written and answers chosen in games, so they can inform later practice and feedback.
02 / Understanding the learner

Find a way of learning that suits you.

We first ask why students are learning Korean and how they prefer to study. We consider their answers, revisions, and teacher observations together to understand their strengths and difficulties.

What information do you use?＋
  • At the start, we ask about learning goals, the preferred order of explanations, when hints are needed, and how students want to receive feedback.
  • We distinguish what students tell us, actual learning records, and AI inferences. We do not judge personality or ability from a single choice or favorite character.
  • We are preparing settings that can change as goals or study preferences change. Students will be able to correct anything the app has misunderstood.
03 / Personalized tasks and feedback

Tasks adapt to your learning record.

We are developing an app that adjusts task topics, hints, and feedback to learners’ goals and records. It is designed to revisit difficult expressions and check whether students can use them without help.

How does learning change?＋
  • We are preparing an approach that shows examples first to students who prefer them, and grammar explanations first to those who want the rule. On this website, you can write your own text, choose the hints you need, and compare your revision with your first draft.
  • We record first drafts, revisions, and hint use separately. The design lets teachers review AI summaries alongside the student’s actual circumstances and arrange further practice of difficult expressions in a new task.
  • We are developing an app that brings together mascot guidance, today’s task, feedback, and learning records. Practice informs the next task and its feedback.

How is a visa interview different from entry screening?＋

A visa interview may be requested by an overseas diplomatic mission during visa assessment. Entry screening is a separate process on arrival in Korea. LAB is preparing educational activities and mock practice to help learners explain their study goals and plans. This is not a visa application agency service or a guarantee of approval. Check the relevant diplomatic mission’s guidance for interview requirements and documents.
