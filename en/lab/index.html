01 / KOREAN THROUGH PLAY

Games where you do what you hear and read

All eight games are free, and you can start without an account. Tap a picture to watch five seconds of real gameplay. The games are in Korean.

GAME 01 · Listening · Reading

Racing

Hear or read a question and steer into the lane with the answer. Covers 2,000 basic words plus sentence, dialogue and notice challenges.

GAME 02 · Listening

Words in Rhythm

Play along to Korean songs we wrote. When a short sentence comes up mid-song, answer O or X.

GAME 03 · Listening

Wind Path

The wind tells you the way in Korean and you move as you hear it — directions, actions and “don’t” — for 90 seconds.

GAME 04 · Listening

Order Rush

Listen to café customers and make their drinks: how many, iced or not, and what to leave out.

GAME 05 · Reading

The Teacher Is Back

Read the teacher’s requests and tidy a messy classroom with Marin and Kkamong, practising patterns like “not ~, but” and “after ~”.

GAME 06 · Reading

Entry Check

Read the notice and let in only the guests who qualify. When you turn someone away, choose the rule they broke.

GAME 07 · Reading

Blank Slice

Read a sentence with a gap and slice only the word piece that fits it.

GAME 08 · Listening

Talk Rally

Mongle speaks Korean as it hits the ball. Pick the right reply to hit it back — the ball waits until you choose.

New to Hangul? Start here

Word Magic, Your First Shop and Fix the Spaceship start with pictures and sound, so you can play before you can read Korean. There is no timer at first.

  1. 01

    You act on what you understand

    Run where you are told, make the drink that was ordered, stamp the ticket the notice allows. The game moves as far as you understood.

  2. 02

    Each round ends with a review

    After a round you replay the sentences you missed or read them again in a new text.

  3. 03

    All eight games share one record

    Atlas uses it to pick your next questions. Sign in with SYNK ID to keep progress in supported games.

The games are basic practice for TOPIK I listening and reading. Speaking and writing are covered in class. Game records are not level assessments and do not guarantee a pass.

Why we built the games and voices this way

Learning a language through games — A meta-analysis by Dixon et al. (2022) reported a positive average effect on second-language learning in studies with comparison groups (d = 0.50). Dixon et al., 2022 ↗

Answer first, then check — Yang et al. (2021) found an average gain from quizzing across 222 studies and 48,478 learners (g = 0.499). Yang et al., 2021 ↗

Pictures and voice together — Combining voice and pictures helps, but research also shows the effect changes when material is complex or fast. So voice lines are short, with optional captions, replay and plenty of time to choose. Reinwein, 2012 ↗

These figures are average effect sizes from outside research, not results measured in SYNK games.

02 / WHY TOPIK 4

The benchmark for studying in Korea: TOPIK 4

We aim for Level 4 with life after admission in mind. Some universities admit students at Level 3, but Level 4 removes many obstacles.

  1. 01

    Graduation

    Some universities require Level 4 or higher to graduate.

  2. 02

    Scholarships

    Some universities offer different scholarships at Level 3 and Level 4.

  3. 03

    Part-time work

    For third- and fourth-year undergraduates, the Korean requirement for part-time work is Level 4.

03 / ONE-YEAR COURSE

In preparation

One year to get ready for study and life in Korea

A full-time, in-person course centred on TOPIK Level 4, covering what you need to follow classes at a Korean university and get by day to day.

  1. 01

    Korean

    Use the grammar and expressions from the textbooks in conversation and games.

  2. 02

    Judgement and teamwork

    Explain the reasons behind your choices in team projects, in Korean.

  3. 03

    Portfolio

    Collect the writing you drafted and revised and the roles you took on.

  4. 04

    Interviews and daily life

    Practise visa interviews and unfamiliar everyday situations before they happen.

In every 90-minute class, 30 minutes are for talking together

Sixty minutes of lessons and practice, thirty minutes of group speaking. In that time you decide what you think first, hear your classmates’ reasons, and then explain your final choice yourself.

  • Decide what you think — Choose before hearing anyone else’s answer.
  • Discuss together — Ask for reasons and agree how everyone takes part.
  • Explain your decision — Say what you kept and what you changed, and why.

Conversation with native speakers

Fix pronunciation and honorifics, and answer unexpected questions in your own words.

English classes

From introductions to reading, email and presentations, taught separately at your level.

Warming up with K-pop

Learn rhythm with a trained dance instructor and pick up movement verbs like “turn” and “stop” with your body.

Even with AI, you make the call

Every eight weeks you choose what to take on next, make it with your team (crew), and keep a portfolio that includes why you revised your work.

ValuesCreativityCooperation and careListening to other viewsChecking AI answers against sourcesExplaining your work in Korean
  1. 01

    Set a goal

    Pick a challenge when you start and every eight weeks.

  2. 02

    Make it together

    Your crew splits the roles and finishes the work.

  3. 03

    Revise

    Use the teacher’s feedback and try again.

  4. 04

    Record the process

    Keep the reasons for each revision in your portfolio.

If you finish the course and do not reach Level 4 on your first TOPIK, we extend your learning app account free for six months.

These are the operating plans for a course in preparation. The conversation textbooks go up to Level 4 and the grammar textbooks up to Level 5. Dates and entry requirements will be in the enrolment notice.

Research and school examples behind the movement classes

We drew on work on exercise and memory (John Ratey of Harvard Medical School; Naperville Central High School), Finland’s Schools on the Move programme, and research on gesture and vocabulary memory (Max Planck Institute, 2015). These informed the class design; they are not measurements of LAB classes.

05 / LEARNING APP

In development

An app that picks your next practice

We are building an app where students use the Korean from their textbooks in games, and the app chooses the next task from their answers and from where they needed help.

  1. 01

    Practise by situation

    Listen in Racing, Words in Rhythm, Wind Path, Order Rush and Talk Rally; read and answer in the story, notice and blank games.

  2. 02

    Find what works for you

    We start with why you are learning and how you like to study, then find what you do well and where you struggle.

  3. 03

    Tasks change with your record

    Expressions you found hard come back for practice, and we check whether you can use them without help.
