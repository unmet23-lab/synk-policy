SYNK ATLAS

Atlas: personalization powered by eight engines

Atlas is SYNK's core technology for finding the learning and support each person needs, based on their goals and experience. Eight engines share the work: understanding the person, choosing what to learn and in what order, explaining it, and reviewing the results.

Select an engine to see what it does.

01 / 08

Core

Choosing the next learning step

Looks at what a learner wants to achieve and where they are struggling, then identifies the next task and the support they need.

02 / 08

Loom

Scenes that respond to you

Combines soft materials, light, and movement to create interfaces and characters, with scenes that respond naturally to the user's actions.

03 / 08

Vellum

Explanations that make sense

Offers explanations and hints suited to the current task and the student's answer. Explains why the practice matters and what to look at when the student gets stuck.

04 / 08

Trail

Tracking practice and progress

Records the tasks completed and the help received. Lets learners compare earlier and recent answers to see what has changed.

05 / 08

Prism

Comparing learning results

Finds recurring difficulties and differences across learning examples. Highlights which content and explanations need a closer look.

06 / 08

Temper

Checking whether support helped

Checks what actually changed after a hint or practice activity, then uses those results to improve tasks and support.

07 / 08

Reed

Music and sound for each scene

Handles music and sound for creative work and learning scenes. Adjusts their use around the listening experience and moments that need focus.

08 / 08 · Planned

Strata

A map of what to learn next

Connects grammar, expressions, and vocabulary by difficulty and prerequisites. Helps identify the learning needed for a goal and which basics to revisit after a mistake.

Atlas · Email writing demo guide

Write an email asking your professor for help

Practice writing for a specific situation, then use the hints you need to improve your own draft.

Your writing task

You were ill and missed an assignment deadline.

The deadline was yesterday. Write an email explaining why you are late and asking your professor for a five-day extension.

The options below preview the help available. Select “Try the email writing demo” to practice yourself.

01 / Plan your message

Plan the three things your email needs.

If you are stuck for words, start with help organizing your message. You do not need to write complete sentences yet.

  1. 01Write down why you could not submit the assignment on time so your professor can understand the situation.
  2. 02Check the extension requested in the task and note how much more time you need.
  3. 03Note the request you want to make: whether your professor can extend the deadline.
Show a sample hint+

Make notes in this order: why you are late → how much extra time you need → your request for an extension. You can make the sentences more polite afterward.

02 / Make a polite request

Check how to make a request to your professor.

Once you know what to say, work on how to say it. Compare your sentences with each point below.

  1. 01Use polite Korean when addressing your professor. Check that none of your sentences end in casual speech.
  2. 02You need your professor's permission to change the deadline. Ask whether an extension is possible rather than simply announcing a new date.
  3. 03Say how much extra time you need so your request is clear. Check that you have included the extension specified in the task.
Show a sample hint+

To ask for permission, you can use a phrase such as “-아/어도 될까요?” (“Would it be okay if I…?”). Try using it in your request for an extension.

03 / Write on your own

Write first, then revise what needs work.

On the first demo screen, choose a friend to give you hints, then select “Start writing” (메일 쓰기 시작). Write in your own words rather than receiving a complete answer.

  1. 01Include why you are late, how much more time you need, and your request. Open hints whenever you need them.
  2. 02When you finish, select “Review and revise my writing” (내 글 확인하고 고치기) to check for missing information and review your tone.
  3. 03After revising, select “Finish my email” (내 메일 완성하기). If you changed your writing, select “Compare my first and revised drafts” (처음 글과 고친 글 나란히 보기) to see the differences side by side.
Show a sample hint+

Read your email aloud. Fix any missing reason or time period, and any request that sounds like a command.

In the demo, you choose the hints you need and revise your own writing. Your email is not sent to a real professor.

When your goal changes, your practice changes too

A student may first prepare for university admission, then need help with presentations or job interviews while studying in Korea. We are developing Atlas to adjust tasks and explanations using the learner's new goals and recent work.

Select a goal to see how the practice changes.

An example of personalization
Study in Korea

Explain your choice of major

You are applying to a Korean university. You need to practice explaining, in Korean, which major interests you and why you want to study it.

  1. 01
    Check your goal and current level

    Briefly explain the major you want to apply for and why. See which expressions you know and where you get stuck.

  2. 02
    Choose the practice you need

    Practice connecting your experience to your chosen major and learn expressions for explaining your reasons.

  3. 03
    Review your next attempt

    Check whether you can explain your major and reasons for applying without hints, and whether a listener can understand you.

Add detail to what you can already say. For expressions you find difficult, start with short sentences.

Class presentations

Explain your findings to classmates

You are giving part of a group presentation at university. You need to explain your findings in order and answer your classmates' questions.

  1. 01
    Check your goal and current level

    Briefly explain your presentation topic. Check whether your ideas connect clearly and which parts are hard to explain.

  2. 02
    Choose the practice you need

    Practice presenting your main points and supporting evidence in order, then answering likely questions in your own words.

  3. 03
    Review your next attempt

    Check whether you can explain the main points without reading from a script and answer follow-up questions.

Once your presentation flows well, practice listening to questions and answering on the spot.

Job interviews

Explain your experience in an interview

You have a job interview coming up. You need to describe your role in a project and give specific examples of how you solved problems.

  1. 01
    Check your goal and current level

    Choose a project you have worked on and explain your role and what you did yourself.

  2. 02
    Choose the practice you need

    Connect the situation, your actions, and the result. Practice follow-up questions an interviewer might ask.

  3. 03
    Review your next attempt

    Check whether you can draw on the same experience to answer different questions and make your own contribution clear.

Keep the experiences you can explain independently, and use questions you found difficult to guide your next practice.

These examples show how practice can change with a learner's goal.

SYNK builds
personalized systems

SYNK builds personalized systems. We develop technology that understands each person's goals, circumstances, and experience and provides the support they need. As their goals and needs change, the system is designed to change with them.

LAB focuses on education and personalized learning; SHIFT on AI training and branding for organizations; PULSE on content and IP; and PATH on study and career preparation in Korea for international learners. Atlas is the shared technology supporting all four.

Understand the person first

We consider each person's goals, current circumstances, strengths, and difficulties. We build systems that use this understanding to suggest relevant learning and support.

Support that fits changing needs

We look beyond the first assessment and take later experiences and choices into account. When goals change or new difficulties arise, learning content and support change too.

Easy to use from the start

We make it easy to find the right features and understand what to do next. We refine every step, from the layout and instructions to the end of the experience.

Keep student work and AI edits distinct

We preserve students' own answers and teachers' observations as original records. AI revisions and inferences are labeled separately so people can review the basis for learning decisions and correct misinterpretations.

Less repetition, more time to learn

Technology helps organize materials and handle repetitive work so people can focus on learning, conversation, and cultural experiences. We want working toward a goal to include the enjoyment of learning and doing things together.

These principles guide the education, services, and content we are preparing. We distinguish plans from current operations. Each brand's page explains what is available now.
