SYNK AtlasPersonalization engine

Atlas fits you better the more you use it

Atlas is the personalization engine SYNK builds. Eight engines understand a person’s goal and situation, fit the content, screens, words and sound to them, and check whether it helped.

See the eight engines ↓SYNK platform ↗

Engines

Eight engines share the work

No single engine decides everything. Some understand, some present, some check — and each service uses the ones it needs.

  1. 01Core

    Decides what is needed now

    Looks at what someone is trying to do and where they are stuck, then decides the next step and the help they need — and records why something was recommended or left out.

    Used in LAB · SHIFT · PATH

  2. 02Strata

    Maps what to learn, in what order

    Links grammar, expressions and vocabulary by difficulty and learning order. It is the basis for deciding which foundations to practise again when a learner gets something wrong.

    Used in LAB

    In preparation
  3. 03Trail

    Records the process and the change

    Records which tasks were done and where help was needed, and compares earlier and recent answers to see what changed.

    Used in LAB · PATH

  4. 04Loom

    Screens that respond to touch

    Brings felt textures to life with light and motion for screens and characters, making scenes that react naturally to what people do.

    Used in LAB · PULSE

  5. 05Vellum

    Explanations people understand

    Gives explanations and hints that fit the current task and the person’s answer — why this practice, and what to look at when stuck.

    Used in LAB · SHIFT

  6. 06Reed

    Music and sound for the scene

    Handles music and sound for works and learning scenes, and decides when to use sound — for example, turning it down when focus matters.

    Used in LAB · PULSE

  7. 07Prism

    Finds patterns across many results

    Finds recurring difficulties and differences across many people’s results, and shows which content and explanations need work.

    Used in LAB · SHIFT

  8. 08Temper

    Checks whether it helped

    After a hint or practice, checks whether the person can now do it alone, and uses that to improve tasks and the kind of help offered.

    Used in LAB · SHIFT · PATH

Flow

Understand, fit, then check

  1. 01

    Understand

    Look at the goal, the current situation and past records — what people told us and what they actually did, never guesses from age or gender.

    Core · Strata · Trail

  2. 02

    Fit

    Fit the content, explanations, screens and sound to the person. They can also choose large text or a sound-free mode.

    Loom · Vellum · Reed

  3. 03

    Check

    Check whether they can now do it without help, and use that for the next suggestion. Methods that make results worse are not rolled out.

    Prism · Temper

By goal

When the goal changes, the practice changes

The same Korean calls for different practice in a university interview, an office meeting, a job interview or a chat with family. Atlas changes the practice by the goal a person sets and their recent results, not by age.

Studying abroad

Explaining your major and why

  1. Check your level — Briefly explain your major and reasons, and find where you get stuck.
  2. Targeted practice — Practise linking your experience to your major.
  3. Try again — Check you can explain it without hints.

At work

Reporting progress in a meeting

  1. Check your level — Say what you did last week and find trouble spots with dates and numbers.
  2. Targeted practice — Put a report in order using common reporting phrases.
  3. Try again — Check you can deliver it in a minute without notes.

Job interview

Getting your experience across

  1. Check your level — Pick one project and say what you did.
  2. Targeted practice — Connect situation, action and result, and prepare for follow-ups.
  3. Try again — Check you can use the same experience for other questions.

Family and neighbours

Asking after someone and sharing news

  1. Check your level — Greet and ask after someone, and see whether choosing honorifics trips you up.
  2. Targeted practice — Say two or three sentences about recent news in the right tone.
  3. Try again — Check you can keep going when they ask back.

These are design examples showing how practice changes with the goal.
